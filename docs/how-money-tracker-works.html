How Money Tracker works

A personal finance app with an accounting-grade ledger underneath and a deliberately simple model on top. Almost every design decision in it follows from one choice — and the two newest features follow from a second.

the one decision

The ledger is ERPNext's, not the app's own

A Transaction — the thing a user actually records — submits a balanced ERPNext Journal Entry, which writes GL Entry rows. That is why the app gets General Ledger, Trial Balance, Balance Sheet, P&L and Cash Flow for free, and why nothing in the app ever adds up money by counting transactions.

the path

What happens when you save an expense

  1. 1 You submit a Transaction

    A voucher: date, amount, account, category. No accounts, no debits, no credits.

  2. 2 The engine asks a strategy which accounts move

    posting/strategies/expense.py takes a context and returns two Leg objects. It touches no database and writes nothing.

  3. 3 The engine checks that debits equal credits

    Before anything is written. ERPNext would catch it too — but then the error names a Journal Entry the user has never heard of.

  4. 4 It builds and submits the Journal Entry

    ERPNext writes the GL Entry rows. This is the only code in the app that writes to the ledger.

  5. 5 Everything downstream is derived

    Balances, the dashboard, the reports, every goal and every budget. Delete the balances cache and the books are still intact.

Why the split between engine and strategy

A strategy is a pure function, so each accounting rule is readable on its own page and testable without a site. The engine does everything risky. The practical consequence: adding a transaction type means adding a strategy, never editing the engine. Five are implemented; nine more are declared and listed as planned, so attempting one fails with a sentence rather than a KeyError.

the rules

Three entries that explain the model

Expense ₹1,000 restaurant bill, paid from HDFC strategies/expense.py
AccountDebitCredit
Restaurants expense1,000.00
HDFC Bank asset1,000.00
1,000.001,000.00

One movement, two sides: spending on Restaurants rises and the bank balance falls by the same amount. Pay by credit card instead and the credit lands on the card's liability account — which is why a card balance grows where a bank balance shrinks.

Transfer ₹10,000 moved from HDFC to SBI strategies/transfer.py
AccountDebitCredit
SBI Bank asset10,000.00
HDFC Bank asset10,000.00
10,000.0010,000.00

Both sides are balance-sheet accounts, so a transfer cannot appear as income or expense — not by policy, but by construction. Moving your own money between pockets is the classic way a naive tracker double-counts spending.

Refund ₹300 refunded against that bill strategies/refund.py
AccountDebitCredit
HDFC Bank asset300.00
Restaurants expense300.00
300.00300.00

The refund credits the category the money was spent on, so net spending on Restaurants reads 700. Booking it as income instead would overstate both income and expense for the month while leaving the bank balance identical — the error would never show up in a reconciliation. That one rule now has exactly one implementation, and the trend chart, the month cards, every goal and every budget all read it from there.

the vocabulary

What each thing signifies

Transaction TXN-00001 · submittable
What the user records — the voucher. Submitting it posts to the ledger; cancelling reverses it. It is never edited after submission and never deleted once posted.
Money Account ACC-00001
Where money sits: a bank, a wallet, a credit card. Backed one-to-one by an ERPNext Account created for it automatically — the user never picks a ledger account.
Category CAT-00001 · tree
What money was for. A group is a heading that holds no ledger account and cannot be posted to, so Food 4,500 is always exactly the sum of its leaves.
Tracker TRK-00001 · accounting dimension
Whose books these are. Registered as an ERPNext Accounting Dimension, so the tracker is stamped on every GL row and every standard report can be filtered by it — which is why the app contains no per-user reporting code at all.
Money Goal GOL-00001 · phase 2
A target and a window — save this much, keep under that, reach this net worth. One window, one deadline, and when it is over it is over.
Money Budget BGT-00001 · phase 2
An envelope that refills every period. Not a goal with a repeat bolted on — see below; the difference is the whole reason it is a separate thing.
Money Settings single
The one place a company, base currency, cost centre and chart-of-accounts parents are resolved. Nothing in the app hardcodes any of them.
Leg
One side of an entry — an account and either a debit or a credit, never both. A frozen dataclass that refuses to exist in an invalid state.
Journal Entry · GL Entry ERPNext
The ledger itself. The Journal Entry is the document; the GL rows are the postings. Cancelling writes reversing rows rather than deleting history.

the second decision

Nothing is stored that can be derived

An account's cached balance is always recomputed from the GL rows, never incremented in place. Phase 2 took that rule and applied it to features that would normally be built the other way round.

A stored counter drifts the first time a transaction is cancelled — and nothing in the data says that it has.

So a goal stores a target and a window, and no progress. A budget stores an amount, a period and a window, and no spending. Every percentage, every outcome word, every carried-over rupee is measured from the ledger at the moment you look at it. Cancel a transaction from three months ago and every figure on every screen is simply correct again, with nothing to reconcile and no job to run.

There is exactly one exception in the whole app, and it is deliberately not about money: a budget records which alert it last sent you, so the daily job can say a thing once instead of every morning.

a boundary

A budget is not a goal that repeats

These two look alike from a distance, and collapsing them into one thing is the obvious shortcut. Keeping them apart is what lets each of them stay simple.

Money Goal

One window, one deadline

₹5,000 on restaurants between April and June. It has a target, it has an end, and it can be achieved or missed exactly once.

  • Savings, into one account
  • Net worth target
  • Income target, over a category
  • Savings rate target
  • Spending limit, over a category
  • Debt payoff, down from an opening

Each kind is one row in a table plus one small function — the same bargain the posting engine makes with its strategies.

Money Budget

An envelope that refills

₹5,000 on restaurants every month, for as long as it is active. It carries two things a goal deliberately has not got.

  • Period — weekly, monthly, quarterly or yearly, aligned to the calendar so a budget's month is the month the dashboard counts
  • Rollover — what is left over moves into the next period, and so does an overspend

That boundary is the reason a goal has no period field, and the reason this is a second service rather than a seventh kind of goal.

The words a budget uses

Derived on read, and ordered by how much they matter. Over Budget wins outright — the money is spent, and the pace cannot argue with it. Then your own alert threshold. Then the pace, which is the only one of the three that can un-say itself: half the envelope gone on day two is a warning, and the same spending on the 31st is a month that worked out.

Not Started No Spend Within Budget At Risk Nearing Limit Over Budget

the seams

Three bridges into ERPNext

Accounts are created, never chosen

Every Money Account and Category gets an ERPNext Account provisioned for it. Users see "HDFC Bank", not a chart of accounts. Matching is by name and root type — ERPNext ships "Salary" as an expense account, so name alone once sent wages into expenses.

One company, many people

Everyone posts into a single shared company, so nothing in the ledger separates one person's books from another's — the permission hooks do. They are the only thing that does, which makes them security code rather than convenience code. Every new tracker-scoped doctype has to be added to them by hand, which is a sharp edge worth knowing about.

A widened Select

Each ledger row points back at the Transaction that caused it. ERPNext ships that field as a fixed list, so a property setter widens it. Without that one patch, nothing could be submitted at all — every strategy failed identically.

the invariants

What keeps it honest

  • The ledger is the truth. Every cached or displayed figure is recomputed from the GL rows.
  • Debits equal credits, checked before anything is written.
  • Transfers and card payments touch only balance-sheet accounts, so they can never surface as income or expense.
  • A refund credits the category — it is not income.
  • History is reversed, not erased. Cancelling writes reversing rows; a posted transaction cannot be deleted.
  • A group category is a heading. Nothing posts to it, and its total is exactly the sum of its leaves.
  • One live envelope per pot of money. Two active budgets over the same category on the same clock are refused, because everything that sums envelopes would count that money twice.
  • Money arithmetic uses precision-aware floats compared at the ledger's own precision, matching ERPNext's idiom rather than fighting it.
